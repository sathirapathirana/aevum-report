Longevity 101

What to expect from a longevity doctor
(and how it's different)

Aevum Protocol 5 min read

A first visit with a longevity doctor doesn't look much like a typical check-up. There's no quick 10-minute chat and a prescription on the way out. Here's what actually happens, step by step.

It starts with data, not a quick chat

Before any real conversation about a plan, a longevity doctor needs a genuinely complete picture — health history, current lifestyle across sleep, nutrition, and movement, existing symptoms or concerns, and what you're actually trying to achieve. This intake is deliberately more detailed than a standard visit, because the plan that follows is only as good as the picture it's built on.

From there, a clinical review covers your current health status directly — vitals, relevant history, and often baseline bloodwork to fill in anything the intake alone can't show.

A personalised plan, not generic advice

Rather than handing over one-size-fits-all guidelines, a longevity doctor builds specific, prioritised targets around your own biology and goals. Not every domain gets equal attention at once — the strongest plans focus on two or three priorities at a time, not six things simultaneously, because that's what actually changes behaviour.

Those targets are usually set across multiple timeframes — what changes in the next 3 months, what's realistic by 6 months, and where things should stand a year out — reflecting how real habits actually form, gradually rather than all at once.

Key Concept

A longevity plan isn't a single document handed to you once. It's a living plan — reviewed, adjusted, and re-prioritised at every follow-up, based on what's actually working and what isn't.

Ongoing tracking, not a one-off appointment

Longevity medicine works over time, not in a single visit. Trends matter more than any one snapshot — which is why regular follow-up, revisited biomarkers, and an updated plan each cycle are central to how it actually functions, not an optional add-on.

  • Regular follow-up visits — reviewing what's working, what isn't, and adjusting the plan accordingly.
  • Tracked biomarkers over time — bloodwork, body composition, and functional markers revisited at intervals, not just once.
  • Direct access between visits — the ability to ask questions as they come up, rather than waiting for the next scheduled appointment.

This is exactly the model Aevum Protocol is built around — a reviewed Health Snapshot, a doctor-built Goals and Actions plan, and a portal that tracks your data between visits, so nothing about your care depends on remembering what was said in one appointment months ago.
